When the 1930 line is busy, Saathi answers

A voice agent for cyber-fraud victims. It listens in Hindi, Hinglish, Punjabi, Tamil or English, collects every number a complaint needs, reads the important ones back, and puts a ready case in front of an officer while the money can still be frozen.

Banks recover far more when a fraud is reported inside the first two hours. Every minute a victim waits on a busy line is money that moves on to the next mule account.

Why it matters

  • ≈ 3.24 crorecalls to 1930 in 2025 (reported), roughly one every second
  • 70–80% vs 15–20%reported recovery inside vs after the first two hours
  • 12 digitsin a UTR. One wrong digit sends the freeze request to the wrong transaction.

What it does

Understands how people actually speak"saade teen hazaar", "dedh lakh", "chaar ek do teen", "double five", on 8 kHz phone audio through Gnani Prisma.
Never trusts a number blindlyUTR, UPI ID and mobile are checked in code, then read back through Gnani Timbre for the caller to confirm.
Stops a scam still in progressIf the caller is on a "digital arrest" video call or has AnyDesk open, safety comes before questions.
Ranks by money still recoverableOfficers see complaints ordered by golden-window time left, with a live countdown.
Links victims of the same muleComplaints paying the same UPI ID or account join one ring the moment a call ends. Repeat reports of one debit are flagged, not double-counted.
Ends with work doneA portal-ready complaint, a bank freeze-request draft and an SMS to the caller, all for officer review.

How a call flows

  1. Prisma transcribes the caller at telephone quality.
  2. The Saathi engine pulls out amount, time, mode, UTR, fraudster ID and bank, checks for a live threat and validates each field.
  3. Timbre asks the next question and reads critical numbers back digit by digit.
  4. Evon v3.3 (optional, self-hosted) writes the officer summary and fills fields the rules miss.
  5. The case lands in the officer console with priority, countdown, ring links and drafts.
